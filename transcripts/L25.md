## Slide 2 — Simple binary hypothesis testing (raster p01)

**Simple binary hypothesis testing**  ("Simple" is highlighted in red on the slide.)

- **null hypothesis** $H_0$:
  $$X \sim p_X(x; H_0) \qquad [\text{or } f_X(x; H_0)]$$
- **alternative hypothesis** $H_1$:
  $$X \sim p_X(x; H_1) \qquad [\text{or } f_X(x; H_1)]$$
- Choose a **rejection region** $R$;
  reject $H_0$ iff data $\in R$

- Likelihood ratio test: reject $H_0$ if
  $$\frac{p_X(x; H_1)}{p_X(x; H_0)} > \xi \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{f_X(x; H_1)}{f_X(x; H_0)} > \xi$$
  - fix false rejection probability $\alpha$
    (e.g., $\alpha = 0.05$)
  - choose $\xi$ so that $\mathbf{P}(\text{reject } H_0; H_0) = \alpha$

Notation note: the semicolon in $p_X(x; H_0)$ and $\mathbf{P}(\cdot\,; H_0)$ denotes "under hypothesis $H_0$" (classical, non-Bayesian: $H_0$ is not a random variable, so it is not a conditioning bar). The threshold symbol is $\xi$ (xi), not a Greek zeta or $\varepsilon$.

## Slide 3 — Example (test on normal mean) (raster p01)

**Example (test on normal mean)**

- $n$ data points, i.i.d.
  $H_0$:  $X_i \sim N(0, 1)$
  $H_1$:  $X_i \sim N(1, 1)$

- Likelihood ratio test; rejection region:
  $$\frac{(1/\sqrt{2\pi})^n \exp\{-\sum_i (X_i - 1)^2/2\}}{(1/\sqrt{2\pi})^n \exp\{-\sum_i X_i^2/2\}} > \xi$$
  - algebra: reject $H_0$ if: $\displaystyle \sum_i X_i > \xi'$  (this conclusion is written in red on the slide)

- Find $\xi'$ such that
  $$\mathbf{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i > \xi'; H_0\Big) = \alpha$$
  - use normal tables

[DERIVATION GAP: the slide says only "algebra" for the step from the likelihood ratio to $\sum_i X_i > \xi'$. The omitted steps: take logs of the ratio, giving $-\tfrac12\sum_i[(X_i-1)^2 - X_i^2] = \sum_i X_i - n/2 > \log \xi$, hence $\sum_i X_i > \log\xi + n/2 =: \xi'$.]

[DERIVATION GAP: "use normal tables" — the slide does not carry out the standardization. Under $H_0$, $\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \sim N(0, n)$, so $\xi' = \sqrt{n}\,z_{1-\alpha}$ where $\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}) = 1-\alpha$; no numerical value is given.]

## Slide 4 — Example (test on normal variance) (raster p01)

**Example (test on normal variance)**

- $n$ data points, i.i.d.
  $H_0$:  $X_i \sim N(0, 1)$
  $H_1$:  $X_i \sim N(0, 4)$

- Likelihood ratio test; rejection region:
  $$\frac{(1/2\sqrt{2\pi})^n \exp\{-\sum_i X_i^2/(2 \cdot 4)\}}{(1/\sqrt{2\pi})^n \exp\{-\sum_i X_i^2/2\}} > \xi$$
  (the "2" in the numerator's normalizing constant $1/2\sqrt{2\pi}$ and the "4" in the exponent denominator $2\cdot 4$ are printed in red, flagging where the variance $\sigma^2 = 4$, $\sigma = 2$, enters)
  - algebra: reject $H_0$ if $\displaystyle \sum_i X_i^2 > \xi'$  (in red on the slide)

- Find $\xi'$ such that
  $$\mathbf{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i^2 > \xi'; H_0\Big) = \alpha$$
  - the distribution of $\sum_i X_i^2$ is known
    (derived distribution problem)
  - "chi-square" distribution;
    tables are available

[DERIVATION GAP: the "algebra" step is skipped. Taking logs: $-n\log 2 - \sum_i X_i^2/8 + \sum_i X_i^2/2 = -n\log 2 + \tfrac{3}{8}\sum_i X_i^2 > \log\xi$, hence $\sum_i X_i^2 > \tfrac{8}{3}(\log\xi + n\log 2) =: \xi'$.]

[DERIVATION GAP: the chi-square distribution of $\sum_{i=1}^n X_i^2$ (with $n$ degrees of freedom, under $H_0$) is asserted, not derived; the slide only labels it a "derived distribution problem".]

## Slide 5 — Composite hypotheses (Is my coin fair?) (raster p02)

**Composite hypotheses**

- Got $S = 472$ heads in $n = 1000$ tosses;
  is the coin fair?
  - $H_0 : p = 1/2$  versus  $H_1 : p \neq 1/2$

- Pick a **"statistic"** (e.g., $S$)
- Pick shape of **rejection region**
  (e.g., $|S - n/2| > \xi$)
- Choose **significance level** (e.g., $\alpha = 0.05$)
- Pick **critical value** $\xi$ so that:
  $$\mathbf{P}(\text{reject } H_0; H_0) = \alpha$$
  Using the CLT:
  $$\mathbf{P}(|S - 500| \le 31; H_0) \approx 0.95; \qquad \xi = 31$$

- In our example: $|S - 500| = 28 < \xi$
  $H_0$ **not rejected** (at the 5% level)

[DERIVATION GAP: the CLT computation giving $\xi = 31$ is not shown. Under $H_0$, $S \sim \text{Binomial}(1000, 1/2)$, mean $500$, standard deviation $\sqrt{1000 \cdot \tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac12} = \sqrt{250} \approx 15.81$; $1.96 \times 15.81 \approx 31$.]

## Slide 6 — Is my die fair? (raster p02)

**Is my die fair?**

- Hypothesis $H_0$:
  $$\mathbf{P}(X = i) = p_i = 1/6, \quad i = 1, \ldots, 6$$
- Observed occurrences of $i$:  $N_i$
- Choose form of rejection region;
  chi-square test:
  $$\text{reject } H_0 \text{ if } \quad T = \sum_i \frac{(N_i - n p_i)^2}{n p_i} > \xi$$
  (the statistic $T$ and its formula are printed in red)

- Choose $\xi$ so that:
  $$\mathbf{P}(\text{reject } H_0; H_0) = 0.05$$
  $$\mathbf{P}(T > \xi; H_0) = 0.05$$

- Need the distribution of $T$:
  (CLT + derived distribution problem)
  - for large $n$, $T$ has approximately
    a chi-square distribution
  - available in tables

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE]
"Is my die fair?" — the slide sets up the full chi-square goodness-of-fit test for a die with $H_0: p_i = 1/6$ for $i = 1,\ldots,6$, observed counts $N_i$, and test statistic $T = \sum_i (N_i - n p_i)^2/(n p_i)$ rejected when $T > \xi$ with $\mathbf{P}(T > \xi; H_0) = 0.05$. No data, no numerical value of $\xi$, and no accept/reject conclusion are given on the slides.

[DERIVATION GAP: the claim that $T$ is approximately chi-square for large $n$ (with $6-1 = 5$ degrees of freedom, a count the slide does not state) is asserted via "CLT + derived distribution problem" with no derivation.]

## Slide 7 — Do I have the correct pdf? (raster p02)

**Do I have the correct pdf?**

- Partition the range into bins
  - $n p_i$: expected incidence of bin $i$
    (from the pdf)
  - $N_i$: observed incidence of bin $i$
  - Use chi-square test (as in die problem)

- Kolmogorov-Smirnov test:
  form **empirical CDF**, $\widehat{F}_X$, from data

### Footnotes on ambiguous symbols

1. The threshold on every slide is the Greek letter $\xi$ (xi), with the derived/transformed threshold written $\xi'$ (xi-prime). It is not a $\zeta$ or an $\varepsilon$; resolved from the standard likelihood-ratio-test notation of Section 9.4 and from the consistent glyph across all slides.
2. The significance level is $\alpha$ (alpha), set to 0.05 in every numerical instance.
3. On Slide 4 the numerator constant reads $1/2\sqrt{2\pi}$, i.e., $1/(2\sqrt{2\pi}) = 1/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$ with $\sigma = 2$; the red "2" and red "4" mark the two places where $H_1$'s variance of 4 enters.
4. On Slide 5, the reported data are $S = 472$ heads out of $n = 1000$, so $|S - 500| = 28$; both digits confirmed from the raster (the plain-text extraction is unreliable for digits).
5. On Slide 7, $\widehat{F}_X$ carries a hat (empirical CDF estimated from data), while $F_X$ is bare (the hypothesized true CDF).
