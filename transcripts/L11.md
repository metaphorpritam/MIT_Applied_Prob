## Slide 2 — A general formula (raster p01)

- Let $Y = g(X)$
  $g$ strictly monotonic.

[FIGURE: Graph illustrating the change-of-variables argument. Axes drawn as a cross with arrowheads: horizontal axis labeled $x$ at its right end, vertical axis labeled $y$ at its top. A single increasing (strictly monotonic) solid curve runs from lower-left to upper-right, gently S-shaped — it rises, flattens slightly through the middle, and steepens sharply again at the far right; a slanted arrow at the right points at the curve from the label $g(x)$ placed to the lower-right of it. Near the middle of the curve a marked point sits on it, and a long slanted arrow comes down to that point from the text label "slope $\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}x}(x)$" printed above the curve (there is no drawn tangent segment — only the arrow annotating the local slope). Two dashed vertical lines drop from the curve down to the $x$-axis, delimiting a short heavy (bold) segment on the $x$-axis; a slanted arrow points up at that segment from the label $[x, x+d]$ printed below the $x$-axis (the "d" is the slide's rendering of $\delta$, i.e. $[x, x+\delta]$). Two dashed horizontal lines run leftward from the curve to the $y$-axis, delimiting a short heavy (bold) segment on the $y$-axis; a slanted arrow points at that segment from the label $[y, y+?]$ printed to its lower-left (the "?" is an unrendered symbol on the slide, i.e. the interval $[y, y+\delta|dg/dx(x)|]$). The picture shows that the small $x$-interval of width $\delta$ maps into a $y$-interval of width $\delta\,|dg/dx(x)|$. | raster/L11_p01.png]

- Event $x \le X \le x+\delta$ is the same as
  $g(x) \le Y \le g(x+\delta)$
  or (approximately)
  $g(x) \le Y \le g(x) + \delta |(dg/dx)(x)|$

- Hence,

$$\delta f_X(x) = \delta f_Y(y) \left| \frac{dg}{dx}(x) \right|$$

  where $y = g(x)$

[DERIVATION GAP: The slide does not carry out the final step of dividing by $\delta$ and solving for $f_Y$, i.e. $f_Y(y) = f_X(x)\big/\left|\frac{dg}{dx}(x)\right|$ with $x = g^{-1}(y)$; nor does it justify the first-order Taylor approximation $g(x+\delta) \approx g(x) + \delta (dg/dx)(x)$, nor treat the strictly-decreasing case explicitly (the absolute value covers it).]

## Slide 3 — The distribution of $X+Y$ (raster p01)

- $W = X + Y$; $X, Y$ independent

[FIGURE: Discrete convolution picture in the $x$–$y$ plane. Horizontal axis labeled $x$, vertical axis labeled $y$ (axes cross at the origin, both positive and negative regions shown). Four labeled dots lie on the anti-diagonal line $x+y=3$: $(0,3)$ on the $y$-axis, $(1,2)$, $(2,1)$, and $(3,0)$ on the $x$-axis. Each label is printed just above-right of its dot. Two additional unlabeled dots continue the same diagonal beyond the ends — one in the second quadrant at $(-1,4)$ (up and to the left of $(0,3)$) and one in the fourth quadrant at $(4,-1)$ (down and to the right of $(3,0)$) — showing that the summation set extends over all integer pairs on the line. The picture depicts the set of $(x,y)$ pairs summing to a fixed value $w$ (here $w=3$), over which the joint PMF is summed. | raster/L11_p01.png]

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_W(w) &= \mathbf{P}(X+Y=w) \\
       &= \sum_x \mathbf{P}(X=x)\mathbf{P}(Y=w-x) \\
       &= \sum_x p_X(x) p_Y(w-x)
\end{aligned}
$$

- Mechanics:
  - Put the pmf's on top of each other
  - Flip the pmf of $Y$
  - Shift the flipped pmf by $w$
    (to the right if $w > 0$)
  - Cross-multiply and add

[DERIVATION GAP: The step from $\mathbf{P}(X+Y=w)$ to $\sum_x \mathbf{P}(X=x)\mathbf{P}(Y=w-x)$ uses the total probability theorem over the values of $X$ together with independence; neither is spelled out on the slide.]

## Slide 4 — The continuous case (raster p01)

- $W = X + Y$; $X, Y$ independent

[FIGURE: Continuous convolution picture in the $x$–$y$ plane. Horizontal axis labeled $x$, vertical axis labeled $y$. A single straight line of slope $-1$ descends from upper-left to lower-right, crossing the $y$-axis high up and the $x$-axis to the right of the origin, and continuing past both crossings (into the second quadrant above-left and the fourth quadrant below-right). The text $x + y = w$ is printed below the line near its lower-right end. Its intercept on the $y$-axis is marked $w$ (label printed to the LEFT of the vertical axis at the crossing height) and its intercept on the $x$-axis is marked $w$ (label printed below the horizontal axis at the crossing, just left of the axis's $x$ label). The line represents the locus of $(x,y)$ with $x+y=w$, along which the joint density is integrated. | raster/L11_p01.png]

- $f_{W|X}(w \mid x) = f_Y(w-x)$

- $f_{W,X}(w,x) = f_X(x) f_{W|X}(w \mid x)$
  $\qquad\qquad\;\; = f_X(x) f_Y(w-x)$

- $$f_W(w) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_X(x) f_Y(w-x)\, dx$$

[DERIVATION GAP: The slide asserts $f_{W|X}(w\mid x) = f_Y(w-x)$ without proof (given $X=x$, $W=x+Y$ is a shift of $Y$, and independence makes the conditional law of $Y$ its unconditional law); the last line marginalizes $f_{W,X}$ over $x$ without stating that step.]

## Slide 5 — Two independent normal r.v.s (raster p02)

- $X \sim N(\mu_x, \sigma_x^2)$, $Y \sim N(\mu_y, \sigma_y^2)$,
  independent

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{X,Y}(x,y) &= f_X(x) f_Y(y) \\
             &= \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_x\sigma_y} \exp\left\{ -\frac{(x-\mu_x)^2}{2\sigma_x^2} - \frac{(y-\mu_y)^2}{2\sigma_y^2} \right\}
\end{aligned}
$$

- PDF is constant on the ellipse where

$$\frac{(x-\mu_x)^2}{2\sigma_x^2} + \frac{(y-\mu_y)^2}{2\sigma_y^2}$$

  is constant

- Ellipse is a circle when $\sigma_x = \sigma_y$

## Slide 6 — The sum of independent normal r.v.'s (raster p02)

- $X \sim N(0, \sigma_x^2)$, $Y \sim N(0, \sigma_y^2)$,
  **independent**

- Let $W = X + Y$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_W(w) &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_X(x) f_Y(w-x)\, dx \\
       &= \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_x\sigma_y} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2/2\sigma_x^2} e^{-(w-x)^2/2\sigma_y^2}\, dx \\
\text{(algebra)} \;\; &= c\, e^{-\gamma w^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

[DERIVATION GAP: The slide writes "(algebra)" and jumps straight to $c\,e^{-\gamma w^2}$ — the completion of the square in $x$, the Gaussian integration over $x$, and the identification of the constants $c$ and $\gamma$ (which give variance $\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2$) are all omitted.]

- Conclusion: **$W$ is normal**
  - mean $= 0$, variance $= \sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2$
  - same argument for nonzero mean case

## Slide 7 — Covariance (raster p02)

- $\mathrm{cov}(X,Y) = \mathbf{E}\Big[(X - \mathbf{E}[X]) \cdot (Y - \mathbf{E}[Y])\Big]$

- Zero-mean case: $\mathrm{cov}(X,Y) = \mathbf{E}[XY]$

[FIGURE: Two side-by-side scatter plots of sample points, each with a horizontal axis labeled $y$ and a vertical axis labeled $x$ (axes drawn as a cross through the origin, all four quadrants visible). LEFT panel: the cloud of dots is elongated along the up-and-to-the-right direction — most points fall in the quadrant where $x$ and $y$ are both positive and the quadrant where both are negative — depicting positive covariance. RIGHT panel: the cloud is elongated along the down-and-to-the-right direction — most points fall where $y>0$, $x<0$ and where $y<0$, $x>0$ — depicting negative covariance. | raster/L11_p02.png]

- $\mathrm{cov}(X,Y) = \mathbf{E}[XY] - \mathbf{E}[X]\mathbf{E}[Y]$

- $$\mathrm{var}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathrm{var}(X_i) + 2\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\,:\, i \neq j}\!\! \mathrm{cov}(X_i, X_j)$$

- independent $\Rightarrow$ $\mathrm{cov}(X,Y) = 0$
  (converse is not true)

[DERIVATION GAP: $\mathrm{cov}(X,Y)=\mathbf{E}[XY]-\mathbf{E}[X]\mathbf{E}[Y]$ is stated without expanding the product in the definition; the variance-of-a-sum identity is stated without derivation.]

*Note on notation: the summation index set on the variance formula is printed $(i,j) : i \neq j$; the leading factor 2 together with an unordered reading of the pairs is the lecturer's convention (equivalently $\sum_{\{i,j\}, i\neq j}$ counted once, doubled).*

## Slide 8 — Correlation coefficient (raster p02)

- Dimensionless version of covariance:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \mathbf{E}\left[ \frac{(X - \mathbf{E}[X])}{\sigma_X} \cdot \frac{(Y - \mathbf{E}[Y])}{\sigma_Y} \right] \\
     &= \frac{\mathrm{cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y}
\end{aligned}
$$

- $-1 \le \rho \le 1$

- $|\rho| = 1 \;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\; (X - \mathbf{E}[X]) = c(Y - \mathbf{E}[Y])$
  (linearly related)

- Independent $\Rightarrow$ $\rho = 0$
  (converse is not true)

[DERIVATION GAP: The bound $-1 \le \rho \le 1$ and the equality condition $|\rho|=1 \Leftrightarrow$ linear relation are asserted without proof (no Cauchy–Schwarz / "variance of a normalized difference is nonnegative" argument on the slide).]
