## Slide 2 — Review (raster p01)

**Review**

- Defining characteristics
  - **Time homogeneity**: $P(k,\tau)$
  - **Independence**
  - **Small interval probabilities** (small $\delta$):

$$P(k,\delta) \approx \begin{cases} 1 - \lambda\delta, & \text{if } k = 0,\\ \lambda\delta, & \text{if } k = 1,\\ 0, & \text{if } k > 1.\end{cases}$$

- $N_\tau$ is a Poisson r.v., with parameter $\lambda\tau$:

$$P(k,\tau) = \frac{(\lambda\tau)^k e^{-\lambda\tau}}{k!}, \qquad k = 0, 1, \ldots$$

$$\mathbf{E}[N_\tau] = \mathrm{var}(N_\tau) = \lambda\tau$$

- Interarrival times ($k = 1$): exponential:

$$f_{T_1}(t) = \lambda e^{-\lambda t}, \quad t \ge 0, \qquad \mathbf{E}[T_1] = 1/\lambda$$

- Time $Y_k$ to $k$th arrival: Erlang($k$):

$$f_{Y_k}(y) = \frac{\lambda^k y^{k-1} e^{-\lambda y}}{(k-1)!}, \qquad y \ge 0$$

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE]
- **Example:**
  Bus interarrival times are equally likely to be 5 or 10 minutes

- If you arrive at a "random time":
  - what is the probability that you selected a 5 minute interarrival interval?
  - what is the expected time to next arrival?

[DERIVATION GAP: no solution on the slide. The length-biasing argument (a randomly chosen time instant lands in a 10-minute interval with probability $10/15 = 2/3$ and in a 5-minute interval with probability $5/15 = 1/3$, giving expected time to next arrival $\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{5}{2} + \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{10}{2} = \frac{5}{6} + \frac{10}{3} = \frac{25}{6}$ minutes) is presented only verbally.]

### Footnotes on ambiguous symbols

1. On Slide 2 the small-interval parameter is $\delta$ (lowercase delta), not a "d"; the interval-length variable in $P(k,\tau)$ is $\tau$ (tau), not "t". The interarrival-time density on the same slide uses $t$ (Roman t) as its argument: $f_{T_1}(t)$.
2. On Slide 2, the Erlang density argument is $y$ and the order is $k$; the exponent reads $y^{k-1}$ and the denominator $(k-1)!$ — confirmed by the standard Erlang form and by the $\lambda^k$ numerator.
3. On Slide 4 the subscripted rates are $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ (lambda, not "n" or "1"); confirmed by the surrounding merging notation.
4. On Slide 6 the split fractions are $p\lambda$ and $(1-p)\lambda$ with $p$ italic; the slide prints the space as "p λ" and "(1 - p)λ".
5. Slide 3 rate is $\lambda = 0.6$/hour (six-tenths per hour). The plain-text extraction of this handout garbles digits; the raster clearly shows "0.6".
