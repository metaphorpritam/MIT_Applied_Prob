## Slide 2 — Types of Inference models/approaches (raster p01)

- Model building versus inferring unknown variables. E.g., assume $X = aS + W$
  - Model building:
    know "signal" $S$, observe $X$, infer $a$
  - Estimation in the presence of noise:
    know $a$, observe $X$, estimate $S$.
- **Hypothesis testing:** unknown takes one of few possible values; aim at small probability of incorrect decision
- **Estimation:** aim at a small estimation error

## Slide 3 — Bayesian inference: Use Bayes rule (raster p01)

- **Hypothesis testing**
  - discrete data

$$p_{\Theta|X}(\theta \mid x) = \frac{p_\Theta(\theta)\, p_{X|\Theta}(x \mid \theta)}{p_X(x)}$$

  - continuous data

$$p_{\Theta|X}(\theta \mid x) = \frac{p_\Theta(\theta)\, f_{X|\Theta}(x \mid \theta)}{f_X(x)}$$

- **Estimation;** continuous data

$$f_{\Theta|X}(\theta \mid x) = \frac{f_\Theta(\theta)\, f_{X|\Theta}(x \mid \theta)}{f_X(x)}$$

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE] The slide then poses a multi-parameter estimation problem (a quadratic trajectory observed in noise) and only states that Bayes rule applies, without carrying out the computation:

$$\begin{aligned}
Z_t &= \Theta_0 + t\Theta_1 + t^2\Theta_2\\
X_t &= Z_t + W_t, \qquad t = 1, 2, \ldots, n
\end{aligned}$$

Bayes rule gives:

$$f_{\Theta_0,\Theta_1,\Theta_2 \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n}(\theta_0, \theta_1, \theta_2 \mid x_1, \ldots, x_n)$$

[DERIVATION GAP: the slide writes down only the name of the posterior $f_{\Theta_0,\Theta_1,\Theta_2\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n}$ — it does not give the explicit Bayes-rule expression (prior $\times$ likelihood / normalizing constant), does not specify the prior on $(\Theta_0,\Theta_1,\Theta_2)$ or the distribution of the noise $W_t$, and does not evaluate the resulting multi-dimensional integral.]

## Slide 4 — Estimation with discrete data (raster p01)

Boxed result (continuous unknown $\Theta$, discrete observation $X$):

$$f_{\Theta|X}(\theta \mid x) = \frac{f_\Theta(\theta)\, p_{X|\Theta}(x \mid \theta)}{p_X(x)}$$

$$p_X(x) = \int f_\Theta(\theta)\, p_{X|\Theta}(x \mid \theta)\, d\theta$$

- **Example:**
  - Coin with unknown parameter $\theta$
  - Observe $X$ heads in $n$ tosses

- What is the Bayesian approach?
  - Want to find $f_{\Theta|X}(\theta \mid x)$
  - Assume a prior on $\Theta$ (e.g., uniform)

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE] Coin with unknown bias $\theta$; $X$ heads are observed in $n$ tosses. Find the posterior $f_{\Theta|X}(\theta\mid x)$ under a prior on $\Theta$ (e.g., uniform on $[0,1]$). The slide poses the problem and outlines the approach only; no posterior is computed.

[DERIVATION GAP: the slide never substitutes the binomial likelihood $p_{X|\Theta}(x\mid\theta) = \binom{n}{x}\theta^x(1-\theta)^{n-x}$ into the boxed formula, nor evaluates the normalizing integral $p_X(x)=\int f_\Theta(\theta)p_{X|\Theta}(x\mid\theta)\,d\theta$.]

## Slide 5 — Output of Bayesian Inference (raster p02)

- Posterior distribution:
  - pmf $p_{\Theta|X}(\cdot \mid x)$ or pdf $f_{\Theta|X}(\cdot \mid x)$

[FIGURE: Two small sketches side by side on a common baseline, illustrating the two forms of the posterior. LEFT: a discrete pmf drawn as five thin vertical spikes of unequal heights rising from a horizontal axis (plain line, arrowhead at the right end), at irregularly spaced abscissas — a stem plot of $p_{\Theta|X}(\cdot\mid x)$. Reading left to right the relative heights are: medium, tallest (about 1.5x the first), short (about half the first), shortest (about a third of the first), then medium again (about equal to the first) after a noticeably wider gap; the axis continues a little past the last spike. RIGHT: a continuous pdf drawn as a smooth bimodal curve over a horizontal axis (arrow pointing right): it rises to a tall narrow peak on the left, dips to a local minimum, rises again to a broader, lower second hump, then decays to zero on the right — a plot of $f_{\Theta|X}(\cdot\mid x)$. Neither sketch carries numeric tick labels. | raster/L21_p02.png]

- If interested in a single answer:
  - Maximum a posteriori probability (MAP):
    - $p_{\Theta|X}(\theta^* \mid x) = \max_\theta p_{\Theta|X}(\theta \mid x)$
      minimizes probability of error;
      often used in hypothesis testing
    - $f_{\Theta|X}(\theta^* \mid x) = \max_\theta f_{\Theta|X}(\theta \mid x)$
  - Conditional expectation:

$$\mathbf{E}[\Theta \mid X = y] = \int \theta f_{\Theta|X}(\theta \mid x)\, d\theta$$

  - Single answers can be misleading!

Footnote: the conditioning variable in the conditional-expectation line is printed as $X = y$ on the slide while the integrand uses $f_{\Theta|X}(\theta\mid x)$; this is a typo on the slide — read it as $\mathbf{E}[\Theta \mid X = x] = \int \theta f_{\Theta|X}(\theta\mid x)\,d\theta$.

- find estimate $c$, to:

$$\text{minimize } \mathbf{E}\!\left[(\Theta - c)^2\right]$$

- Optimal estimate: $c = \mathbf{E}[\Theta]$

$$\mathbf{E}\!\left[(\Theta - \mathbf{E}[\Theta])^2\right] = \mathrm{Var}(\Theta)$$

### Footnotes on ambiguous symbols

1. $\Theta$ (capital theta) denotes the *random* unknown in the Bayesian setting; $\theta$ (lowercase theta) denotes its numerical value / the non-random unknown parameter in classical statistics. $\hat{\Theta}$ denotes the estimator output. These are distinguished consistently throughout; the garbled text extraction renders many of them as bare boxes and cannot be trusted.
2. The noise input into the block diagrams is labeled $N$ (capital N, for noise), not $n$; the number of observations/tosses is lowercase $n$.
3. Slide 6's uniform-pdf sketch carries no numbers at all on the printed slide — no height, no interval endpoints, no axis names. Any specific values (e.g., a height $1/6$ over $[4,10]$) would have been supplied verbally in lecture and are **not** in the handout; do not attribute them to the slide.
4. Slide 3's model uses $\Theta_0,\Theta_1,\Theta_2$ (subscripts 0,1,2) and $t^2$; the extracted text garbles these as h, i, k.
5. Red ink is used on the slides for the key items: $X = aS + W$ (Slide 2); all three Bayes-rule displays and the posterior name $f_{\Theta_0,\Theta_1,\Theta_2\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n}$ (Slide 3); the MAP pmf line $p_{\Theta|X}(\theta^*\mid x)=\max_\theta p_{\Theta|X}(\theta\mid x)$ (Slide 5, while the pdf line just below it is in black); the entire red-outlined conclusion box (Slide 7); and $\mathbf{E}[\Theta \mid X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ (Slide 8).
