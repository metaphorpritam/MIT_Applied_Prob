## Slide 2 — Chebyshev's inequality (raster p01)

- Random variable $X$
(with finite mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma^2 \;&=\; \int (x-\mu)^2 f_X(x)\,dx \\[4pt]
&\ge\; \int_{-\infty}^{-c} (x-\mu)^2 f_X(x)\,dx \;+\; \int_{c}^{\infty} (x-\mu)^2 f_X(x)\,dx \\[4pt]
&\ge\; c^2 \cdot \mathbf{P}(|X-\mu| \ge c)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$\mathbf{P}(|X-\mu| \ge c) \;\le\; \frac{\sigma^2}{c^2}$$

$$\mathbf{P}(|X-\mu| \ge k\sigma) \;\le\; \frac{1}{k^2}$$

[DERIVATION GAP: The integration limits are printed on the slide exactly as $\int_{-\infty}^{-c}$ and $\int_{c}^{\infty}$. Strictly, the region $\{|x-\mu|\ge c\}$ is $(-\infty,\mu-c]\cup[\mu+c,\infty)$; the slide is written as if $\mu = 0$ (or is simply shorthand). The step from line 1 to line 2 also silently uses that the integrand $(x-\mu)^2 f_X(x)$ is nonnegative so dropping the middle region only decreases the integral. The step from line 2 to line 3 lower-bounds $(x-\mu)^2$ by $c^2$ on the retained region and recognizes the remaining integral as $\mathbf{P}(|X-\mu|\ge c)$. The final line is obtained by substituting $c = k\sigma$ into $\sigma^2/c^2$.]

## Slide 3 — Deterministic limits (raster p01)

- Sequence $a_n$
Number $a$

- $a_n$ converges to $a$
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = a$$
"$a_n$ eventually gets and stays (arbitrarily) close to $a$"

- For every $\epsilon > 0$,
there exists $n_0$,
such that for every $n \ge n_0$,
we have $|a_n - a| \le \epsilon$.

## Slide 4 — Convergence "in probability" (raster p01)

- Sequence of random variables $Y_n$

- converges in probability to a number $a$:
"(almost all) of the PMF/PDF of $Y_n$, eventually gets concentrated (arbitrarily) close to $a$"

- For every $\epsilon > 0$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \mathbf{P}(|Y_n - a| \ge \epsilon) = 0$$

## Slide 5 — Convergence of the sample mean (Weak law of large numbers) (raster p02)

**Convergence of the sample mean**
(Weak law of large numbers)

- $X_1, X_2, \dots$ i.i.d.
finite mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$
$$M_n = \frac{X_1 + \cdots + X_n}{n}$$

- $\mathbf{E}[M_n] =$
(left blank on the slide — to be filled in during lecture; the value is $\mu$)

- $\mathrm{Var}(M_n) =$
(left blank on the slide — to be filled in during lecture; the value is $\sigma^2/n$)

$$\mathbf{P}(|M_n - \mu| \ge \epsilon) \;\le\; \frac{\mathrm{Var}(M_n)}{\epsilon^2} \;=\; \frac{\sigma^2}{n\epsilon^2}$$

- $M_n$ converges in probability to $\mu$

[DERIVATION GAP: The slide leaves $\mathbf{E}[M_n]$ and $\mathrm{Var}(M_n)$ as blanks to be filled in live, yet the displayed Chebyshev bound already uses $\mathrm{Var}(M_n) = \sigma^2/n$ (which follows from independence: $\mathrm{Var}(X_1+\cdots+X_n) = n\sigma^2$, divided by $n^2$). The final conclusion also skips the step of letting $n \to \infty$ so that $\sigma^2/(n\epsilon^2) \to 0$ for every fixed $\epsilon > 0$.]

## Slide 6 — The pollster's problem (raster p02)

- $f$: fraction of population that "..."

- $i$th (randomly selected) person polled:
$$X_i = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if yes,} \\ 0, & \text{if no.} \end{cases}$$

- $M_n = (X_1 + \cdots + X_n)/n$
fraction of "yes" in our sample

- Goal: 95% confidence of $\le 1\%$ error
$$\mathbf{P}(|M_n - f| \ge .01) \le .05$$

- Use Chebyshev's inequality:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{P}(|M_n - f| \ge .01) \;&\le\; \frac{\sigma^2_{M_n}}{(0.01)^2} \\[4pt]
&=\; \frac{\sigma^2_x}{n(0.01)^2} \;\le\; \frac{1}{4n(0.01)^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

- If $n = 50{,}000$,
then $\mathbf{P}(|M_n - f| \ge .01) \le .05$
(conservative)

[DERIVATION GAP: Three steps are skipped. (i) $\mathbf{E}[M_n] = f$, so Chebyshev applies with mean $f$. (ii) $\sigma^2_{M_n} = \sigma^2_x/n$ where $\sigma^2_x = \mathrm{Var}(X_i) = f(1-f)$. (iii) The bound $\sigma^2_x \le 1/4$ comes from $\max_{0\le f\le 1} f(1-f) = 1/4$. The final numeric claim is not verified on the slide: $1/(4 \cdot 50000 \cdot (0.01)^2) = 1/20 = 0.05$.]

Footnote on notation: the slide writes the sample variance symbol with a lowercase subscript, $\sigma^2_x$ (i.e. the per-person variance $\mathrm{Var}(X_i)$), distinct from $\sigma^2_{M_n}$ on the line above.

## Slide 8 — The central limit theorem (raster p02)

- "Standardized" $S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n$:
$$Z_n = \frac{S_n - \mathbf{E}[S_n]}{\sigma_{S_n}} = \frac{S_n - n\mathbf{E}[X]}{\sqrt{n}\,\sigma}$$
  - zero mean
  - unit variance

- Let $Z$ be a standard normal r.v.
(zero mean, unit variance)

- **Theorem:** For every $c$:
$$\mathbf{P}(Z_n \le c) \to \mathbf{P}(Z \le c)$$

- $\mathbf{P}(Z \le c)$ is the standard normal CDF, $\Phi(c)$, available from the normal tables

[DERIVATION GAP: The equality of the two expressions for $Z_n$ uses $\mathbf{E}[S_n] = n\mathbf{E}[X]$ and $\sigma_{S_n} = \sqrt{n}\,\sigma$ (from $\mathrm{Var}(S_n) = n\sigma^2$), which is not shown. The claims "zero mean" and "unit variance" are stated without verification, and the theorem itself is stated without proof; the limit is as $n \to \infty$ (the slide writes only "$\to$").]
