## Slide 2 — Continuous r.v.'s and pdf's (raster p01)

- **A continuous r.v.** is described by a **probability density function** $f_X$

[FIGURE: Two-part schematic, side by side. LEFT: a large shaded (grey) oval representing the sample space, with a small white ellipse drawn inside it; a thin leader line runs from the small ellipse to the shaded region of the plot on the right. The small ellipse is labelled below by an arrow: "Event {a ≤ X ≤ b }". RIGHT: a plot with a vertical axis labelled f_X(x) and a horizontal axis labelled x. The curve is a smooth bimodal density: it rises from 0 just after the origin to a modest first hump, dips, then rises to a taller second hump further right, then decays back to 0. Two tick marks a and b are on the x-axis (a left of b, both under the first hump region); the area under the curve between a and b is filled with diagonal hatching, depicting P(a ≤ X ≤ b) as that area. | raster/L08_p01.png]

$$\mathbf{P}(a \le X \le b) = \int_a^b f_X(x)\,dx$$

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_X(x)\,dx = 1$$

$$\mathbf{P}(x \le X \le x+\delta) = \int_x^{x+\delta} f_X(s)\,ds \approx f_X(x)\cdot\delta$$

$$\mathbf{P}(X \in B) = \int_B f_X(x)\,dx, \quad \text{for “nice” sets } B$$

## Slide 3 — Means and variances (raster p01)

- $\mathbf{E}[X] = \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x f_X(x)\,dx$

- $\mathbf{E}[g(X)] = \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(x) f_X(x)\,dx$

- $\mathrm{var}(X) = \sigma_X^2 = \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (x - \mathbf{E}[X])^2 f_X(x)\,dx$

- **Continuous Uniform r.v.**

Ticks a and b are marked on the x-axis; the constant height is not numerically labelled on the slide (it is the blank the lecturer fills in as 1/(b−a)). | raster/L08_p01.png]

## Slide 4 — Cumulative distribution function (CDF) (raster p01)

**Cumulative distribution function (CDF)**

$$F_X(x) = \mathbf{P}(X \le x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} f_X(t)\,dt$$

[FIGURE: Two side-by-side plots for a continuous uniform r.v. LEFT plot: vertical axis labelled f_X(x), horizontal axis labelled x; the pdf is 0 up to tick a, jumps to a constant height forming a rectangle between ticks a and b, then drops back to 0 — the uniform density. RIGHT plot: vertical axis labelled CDF, horizontal axis labelled x; the curve is 0 (flat on the axis) up to tick a, then rises as a straight line (ramp) from a to b, then is flat at its maximum level for x > b (that level is the value 1, but no numeric label "1" is printed on the slide — the vertical axis carries only the word CDF). Ticks a and b are aligned between the two plots. | raster/L08_p01.png]

- Also for discrete r.v.'s:

$$F_X(x) = \mathbf{P}(X \le x) = \sum_{k \le x} p_X(k)$$

[FIGURE: Two side-by-side plots for a discrete r.v. LEFT plot: a PMF bar/stem chart, x-axis with ticks at 1, 2, 4 (axis labelled x). Three vertical spikes: at x = 1 of height labelled 1/6, at x = 2 of height labelled 3/6 (tallest), and at x = 4 of height labelled 2/6 (intermediate). RIGHT plot: the corresponding CDF as a right-continuous staircase, x-axis with ticks at 1, 2, 4. The function is 0 to the left of 1, jumps at x = 1 and stays flat, jumps again at x = 2 and stays flat, then jumps again at x = 4 and stays flat thereafter. The step levels are NOT numerically labelled on the slide (unlike the PMF spikes, which are); from the PMF they are 1/6, 4/6 and 1 respectively, and the jump heights equal the PMF values 1/6, 3/6, 2/6. The staircase is drawn right-continuous, and this right-hand plot has no vertical axis drawn — only a horizontal x-axis with ticks 1, 2, 4. | raster/L08_p01.png]

### Footnotes on ambiguous symbols

1. Slide 2, the approximation bullet: the small increment symbol is $\delta$ (lowercase delta), not $\partial$ or $d$ — confirmed by its use as an interval width in $[x, x+\delta]$ and multiplication $f_X(x)\cdot\delta$.
2. Slide 6: the parameters of the general normal are $\mu$ (mean) and $\sigma^2$ (variance); the exponent reads $-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2$ and the prefactor $1/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$. Resolved from mathematical context (standard Gaussian density).
3. Slide 5: the horizontal axis of the first mixed-distribution figure is rendered with a subscript, i.e. "x_0"; it functions simply as the value axis for $x$.
4. Slide 6 figure: a small stray glyph — it reads like a smiley ":)" — sits in the white space *between* the two plots (to the right of the bell curve, left of and above the CDF plot). It is an artifact of the original slide and carries no mathematical content. [UNCERTAIN: exact glyph; best reading ":)"]
5. Slide 7: the last expression is written as "CDF(0.25)" using an upright sans-serif "CDF", meaning the standard normal CDF $\Phi$ evaluated at 0.25.
