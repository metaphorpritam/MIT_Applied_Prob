## Slide 2 — Random variables (raster p01)

- An assignment of a value (number) to every possible outcome
- Mathematically: A function from the sample space $\Omega$ to the real numbers
  - discrete or continuous values
- Can have several random variables defined on the same sample space
- Notation:
  - random variable $X$
  - numerical value $x$

## Slide 3 — Probability mass function (PMF) (raster p01)

- ("probability law", "probability distribution" of $X$)

- Notation:

$$p_X(x) \;=\; \mathbf{P}(X = x)$$
$$\phantom{p_X(x)} \;=\; \mathbf{P}(\{\omega \in \Omega \ \text{s.t.} \ X(\omega) = x\})$$

- $p_X(x) \ge 0 \qquad \sum_x p_X(x) = 1$

- **Example:** $X$ = number of coin tosses until first head
  - assume independent tosses, $\mathbf{P}(H) = p > 0$

$$p_X(k) \;=\; \mathbf{P}(X = k)$$
$$\phantom{p_X(k)} \;=\; \mathbf{P}(TT\cdots TH)$$
$$\phantom{p_X(k)} \;=\; (1-p)^{k-1} p, \qquad k = 1, 2, \ldots$$

  - **geometric** PMF

[DERIVATION GAP: The step from $\mathbf{P}(TT\cdots TH)$ (a sequence of $k-1$ tails followed by one head) to $(1-p)^{k-1}p$ uses independence of the tosses to multiply the per-toss probabilities; the slide states the result without writing the product out. Normalization $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-p)^{k-1}p = 1$ is also asserted only implicitly via the general property above.]

## Slide 4 — How to compute a PMF $p_X(x)$ (raster p01)

- collect all possible outcomes for which $X$ is equal to $x$
- add their probabilities
- repeat for all $x$

- **Example:** Two independent rools [sic — "rolls"] of a fair tetrahedral die

  $F$: outcome of first throw
  $S$: outcome of second throw
  $X = \min(F, S)$

## Slide 5 — Binomial PMF (raster p02)

- $X$: number of heads in $n$ independent coin tosses
- $\mathbf{P}(H) = p$
- Let $n = 4$

$$p_X(2) \;=\; \mathbf{P}(HHTT) + \mathbf{P}(HTHT) + \mathbf{P}(HTTH)$$
$$\phantom{p_X(2) =} \; +\, \mathbf{P}(THHT) + \mathbf{P}(THTH) + \mathbf{P}(TTHH)$$
$$\phantom{p_X(2)} \;=\; 6 p^2 (1-p)^2$$
$$\phantom{p_X(2)} \;=\; \binom{4}{2} p^2 (1-p)^2$$

In general:

$$p_X(k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}, \qquad k = 0, 1, \ldots, n$$

[DERIVATION GAP: Each of the six listed sequences is assigned probability $p^2(1-p)^2$ by independence without that intermediate line being shown; and the jump from the $n=4$, $k=2$ case to the general formula $\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ is asserted, not derived (the counting argument that there are $\binom{n}{k}$ sequences with $k$ heads is omitted).]

## Slide 8 — Variance (raster p02)

Recall: $\displaystyle \mathbf{E}[g(X)] = \sum_x g(x) p_X(x)$

- **Second moment:** $\mathbf{E}[X^2] = \sum_x x^2 p_X(x)$

- **Variance**

$$\mathrm{var}(X) \;=\; \mathbf{E}\!\left[(X - \mathbf{E}[X])^2\right]$$
$$\phantom{\mathrm{var}(X)} \;=\; \sum_x (x - \mathbf{E}[X])^2 p_X(x)$$
$$\phantom{\mathrm{var}(X)} \;=\; \mathbf{E}[X^2] - (\mathbf{E}[X])^2$$

**Properties**:

- $\mathrm{var}(X) \ge 0$
- $\mathrm{var}(\alpha X + \beta) = \alpha^2 \mathrm{var}(X)$

[DERIVATION GAP: The identity $\mathbf{E}[(X-\mathbf{E}[X])^2] = \mathbf{E}[X^2] - (\mathbf{E}[X])^2$ is stated with no expansion of the square / use of linearity of expectation. Likewise $\mathrm{var}(\alpha X + \beta) = \alpha^2\mathrm{var}(X)$ is given without proof.]
