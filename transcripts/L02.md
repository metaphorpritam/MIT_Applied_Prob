## Slide 6 — Multiplication rule (raster p02)

$$\mathbf{P}(A \cap B \cap C) = \mathbf{P}(A) \cdot \mathbf{P}(B \mid A) \cdot \mathbf{P}(C \mid A \cap B)$$

## Slide 7 — Total probability theorem (raster p02)

- Divide and conquer
- Partition of sample space into $A_1, A_2, A_3$
- Have $\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_i)$, for every $i$

[FIGURE: A rectangle representing the sample space (no $\Omega$ label on the slide), divided into three regions by two curves:
 (i) a curve entering from the left edge (a little above the bottom) and sweeping up to the right to meet the TOP edge — it separates $A_1$ (the upper-left region) from $A_2$ (the lower-left region);
 (ii) a second curve running from the top edge (right of centre) down, bowing slightly left then right, to the BOTTOM edge — everything to the right of it is $A_3$.
Region labels "A_1" (upper-left corner, subscripted), "A_2" (lower-left corner), "A_3" (lower-right corner) are printed inside the rectangle in upright roman type.
A grey-shaded horizontal ellipse occupying the middle of the rectangle is the event $B$; note the ellipse carries NO letter label on the slide. It straddles all three regions, so $B$ is cut into the three pieces $A_1\cap B$, $A_2\cap B$, $A_3\cap B$; both dividing curves pass through the shaded ellipse. | raster/L02_p02.png]

- One way of computing $\mathbf{P}(B)$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{P}(B) = \; & \mathbf{P}(A_1)\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_1) \\
+ \; & \mathbf{P}(A_2)\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_2) \\
+ \; & \mathbf{P}(A_3)\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_3)
\end{aligned}$$

*(The displayed formula is printed in red.)*

[DERIVATION GAP: The slide states the result without deriving it — the steps $\mathbf{P}(B)=\sum_i \mathbf{P}(A_i \cap B)$ (finite additivity over the partition) followed by $\mathbf{P}(A_i\cap B)=\mathbf{P}(A_i)\mathbf{P}(B\mid A_i)$ are not shown.]

## Slide 8 — Bayes' rule (raster p02)

- "Prior" probabilities $\mathbf{P}(A_i)$
  - initial "beliefs"
- We know $\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_i)$ for each $i$
- Wish to compute $\mathbf{P}(A_i \mid B)$
  - revise "beliefs", given that $B$ occurred

[FIGURE: The identical partition diagram as on the Total probability theorem slide, redrawn — a rectangle (sample space, unlabeled) split by two curves: one from the left edge sweeping up to the top edge (separating $A_1$ upper-left from $A_2$ lower-left), and one from the top edge running down to the bottom edge (cutting off $A_3$ on the right). Labels "A_1" (upper-left), "A_2" (lower-left), "A_3" (lower-right). A grey-shaded horizontal ellipse in the middle is the event $B$ (again carrying no letter label), overlapping all three regions. | raster/L02_p02.png]

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{P}(A_i \mid B) &= \frac{\mathbf{P}(A_i \cap B)}{\mathbf{P}(B)} \\[4pt]
&= \frac{\mathbf{P}(A_i)\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_i)}{\mathbf{P}(B)} \\[4pt]
&= \frac{\mathbf{P}(A_i)\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_i)}{\sum_j \mathbf{P}(A_j)\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_j)}
\end{aligned}$$

*(The left-hand side $\mathbf{P}(A_i\mid B)$ and the middle line of the derivation are printed in red.)*

[DERIVATION GAP: The last equality silently substitutes the total probability theorem $\mathbf{P}(B)=\sum_j \mathbf{P}(A_j)\mathbf{P}(B\mid A_j)$ into the denominator; this substitution step is not spelled out.]

### Notes on ambiguous symbols

- The superscript on complements is a lowercase italic "c" (e.g. $A^c$, $B^c$, $C^c$), rendered in the tree figures as "A^c", "B^c", "C^c".
- On Slide 4 the grid is 4×4 (a four-sided die), not 6×6; the axis ticks read 1, 2, 3, 4 in both directions.
- Radar-example numerals were read directly from the raster: 0.05, 0.95, 0.99, 0.01, 0.10, 0.90. These are internally consistent (0.99+0.01=1, 0.10+0.90=1, 0.05+0.95=1).
- The readings on Slide 1 read "Sections 1.3-1.4"; the extracted text of the PDF garbles these digits and should not be trusted.
- In the partition diagrams on Slides 7 and 8 the grey ellipse is **not** labeled "B" on the slide; the identification of the ellipse with the event $B$ comes from the surrounding text ("One way of computing $\mathbf{P}(B)$", "given that $B$ occurred"), not from the drawing.
- Neither the rectangle on Slide 3 nor those on Slides 7–8 carries an $\Omega$ label; the sample-space reading is inferred.
- On Slide 6 the $A^c$ half of the tree is drawn incompletely: the lower $A^c$ branch is a bare stub with no second/third-stage branching, and the $A\cap B\cap C^c$ leaf is present as a line but has no text.
- On Slide 6 the multiplication-rule formula uses centred multiplication dots: $\mathbf{P}(A\cap B\cap C)=\mathbf{P}(A)\cdot\mathbf{P}(B\mid A)\cdot\mathbf{P}(C\mid A\cap B)$ — note the conditioning in the last factor is on $A\cap B$ (both events), not on $B$ alone.
- On Slide 8 the red highlighting covers the left-hand side $\mathbf{P}(A_i\mid B)$ and the **entire** second fraction (numerator $\mathbf{P}(A_i)\mathbf{P}(B\mid A_i)$, fraction bar, and denominator $\mathbf{P}(B)$); the first and third fractions are in black.
