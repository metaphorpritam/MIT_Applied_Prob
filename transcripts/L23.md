## Slide 3 — Maximum Likelihood Estimation (raster p01)

- Model, with unknown parameter(s):
  $$X \sim p_X(x;\theta)$$

- Pick $\theta$ that "makes data most likely"
  *(in red)*
  $$\hat\theta_{\mathsf{ML}} = \arg\max_{\theta} p_X(x;\theta)$$

- Compare to Bayesian MAP estimation:
  $$\hat\theta_{\mathsf{MAP}} = \arg\max_{\theta} p_{\Theta\mid X}(\theta \mid x)$$
  $$\hat\theta_{\mathsf{MAP}} = \arg\max_{\theta} \frac{p_{X\mid\Theta}(x\mid\theta)\, p_{\Theta}(\theta)}{p_X(x)}$$

- **Example:** $X_1,\dots,X_n$: i.i.d., exponential($\theta$)

$$\max_{\theta} \prod_{i=1}^{n} \theta e^{-\theta x_i}$$

$$\max_{\theta} \left( n\log\theta - \theta \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \right)$$

$$\hat\theta_{\mathsf{ML}} = \frac{n}{x_1 + \cdots + x_n} \qquad \hat\Theta_n = \frac{n}{X_1 + \cdots + X_n}$$

*(the last expression $\hat\Theta_n$, the estimator viewed as a random variable, is printed in red)*

[DERIVATION GAP: The slide jumps from the likelihood product $\prod_{i=1}^n \theta e^{-\theta x_i}$ to the log-likelihood $n\log\theta - \theta\sum_i x_i$ without writing the intermediate step $\theta^n e^{-\theta\sum_i x_i}$ or noting that $\log$ is monotone so maximizing the log is equivalent. It then states the maximizer $n/(x_1+\cdots+x_n)$ without showing the differentiation $\frac{d}{d\theta}\big(n\log\theta - \theta\sum x_i\big) = n/\theta - \sum x_i = 0$, nor checking the second-order/concavity condition.]

## Slide 4 — Desirable properties of estimators (should hold FOR ALL $\theta$ !!!) (raster p01)

- **Unbiased:** $\mathbf{E}[\hat\Theta_n] = \theta$
  - exponential example, with $n = 1$:
    $\mathbf{E}[1/X_1] = \infty \neq \theta$
    (biased)

- **Consistent:** $\hat\Theta_n \to \theta$ (in probability)
  - exponential example:
    $(X_1 + \cdots + X_n)/n \to \mathbf{E}[X] = 1/\theta$
  - can use this to show that:
    $\hat\Theta_n = n/(X_1 + \cdots + X_n) \to 1/\mathbf{E}[X] = \theta$

- **"Small" mean squared error (MSE)**
  $$\mathbf{E}[(\hat\Theta - \theta)^2] \;=\; \operatorname{var}(\hat\Theta - \theta) + \big(\mathbf{E}[\hat\Theta - \theta]\big)^2$$
  $$=\; \operatorname{var}(\hat\Theta) + (\text{bias})^2$$

[DERIVATION GAP: The claim $\mathbf{E}[1/X_1] = \infty$ for an exponential($\theta$) random variable is asserted without the integral $\int_0^\infty (1/x)\,\theta e^{-\theta x}\,dx = \infty$ (divergence at $x\to 0$). The consistency step from $(X_1+\cdots+X_n)/n \to 1/\theta$ to $n/(X_1+\cdots+X_n)\to\theta$ invokes, without stating it, the fact that convergence in probability is preserved under a continuous function ($g(t)=1/t$). The MSE decomposition uses $\operatorname{var}(\hat\Theta-\theta)=\operatorname{var}(\hat\Theta)$ (since $\theta$ is a constant) and the identity $\mathbf{E}[Y^2]=\operatorname{var}(Y)+(\mathbf{E}[Y])^2$ without stating either step.]

## Slide 5 — Estimate a mean (raster p02)

- $X_1,\dots,X_n$: i.i.d., mean $\theta$, variance $\sigma^2$

$$X_i = \theta + W_i$$

$W_i$: i.i.d., mean, 0, variance $\sigma^2$

$$\hat\Theta_n = \text{sample mean} = M_n = \frac{X_1 + \cdots + X_n}{n}$$

**Properties:**

- $\mathbf{E}[\hat\Theta_n] = \theta$   (unbiased)
- WLLN: $\hat\Theta_n \to \theta$   (consistency)
- MSE: $\sigma^2/n$

- Sample mean often turns out to also be the ML estimate.
  E.g., if $X_i \sim N(\theta,\sigma^2)$, i.i.d.

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE]
E.g., if $X_i \sim N(\theta,\sigma^2)$, i.i.d.: show that the sample mean $M_n = (X_1+\cdots+X_n)/n$ is also the maximum likelihood estimate of $\theta$. The slide states this fact but carries out no maximization of the Gaussian likelihood.

[DERIVATION GAP: The three listed properties (unbiasedness $\mathbf{E}[M_n]=\theta$, consistency by the weak law of large numbers, and $\mathrm{MSE}=\sigma^2/n$) are asserted with no computation; in particular $\operatorname{var}(M_n)=\sigma^2/n$ from independence is not shown, and since $M_n$ is unbiased the MSE equals that variance.]

## Slide 6 — Confidence intervals (CIs) (raster p02)

- An estimate $\hat\Theta_n$ may not be informative enough

- An $1-\alpha$ **confidence interval** is a (random) interval $[\hat\Theta_n^-, \hat\Theta_n^+]$, *(interval printed in red)*

  s.t.  *(in red)*  $\mathbf{P}(\hat\Theta_n^- \le \theta \le \hat\Theta_n^+) \ge 1-\alpha, \quad \forall\, \theta$

  - often $\alpha = 0.05$, or $0.25$, or $0.01$
  - interpretation is subtle

- CI in estimation of the mean
  $\hat\Theta_n = (X_1 + \cdots + X_n)/n$
  - normal tables: $\Phi(1.96) = 1 - 0.05/2$

$$\mathbf{P}\!\left(\frac{|\hat\Theta_n - \theta|}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} \le 1.96\right) \approx 0.95 \quad (\text{CLT})$$

$$\mathbf{P}\!\left(\hat\Theta_n - \frac{1.96\,\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \le \theta \le \hat\Theta_n + \frac{1.96\,\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right) \approx 0.95$$

More generally: let $z$ be s.t. *(in red)* $\Phi(z) = 1 - \alpha/2$

*(in red)*
$$\mathbf{P}\!\left(\hat\Theta_n - \frac{z\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \le \theta \le \hat\Theta_n + \frac{z\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right) \approx 1-\alpha$$

[DERIVATION GAP: The CLT step is only named, not carried out — that $(\hat\Theta_n-\theta)/(\sigma/\sqrt{n})$ is approximately standard normal for large $n$, so that $\mathbf{P}(|Z|\le 1.96)=2\Phi(1.96)-1\approx 0.95$. The algebraic rearrangement of the first probability statement into the interval form on the second line is also left to the reader.]

## Slide 7 — The case of unknown $\sigma$ (raster p02)

- Option 1: use upper bound on $\sigma$
  - if $X_i$ Bernoulli: $\sigma \le 1/2$

- Option 2: use ad hoc estimate of $\sigma$
  - if $X_i$ Bernoulli($\theta$): $\hat\sigma = \sqrt{\hat\Theta(1-\hat\Theta)}$

- Option 3: Use generic estimate of the variance
  - Start from $\sigma^2 = \mathbf{E}[(X_i - \theta)^2]$

$$\hat\sigma_n^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i - \theta)^2 \;\to\; \sigma^2$$

(but do not know $\theta$)

$$\hat S_n^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i - \hat\Theta_n)^2 \;\to\; \sigma^2$$

(unbiased: $\mathbf{E}[\hat S_n^2] = \sigma^2$)

[DERIVATION GAP: The bound $\sigma \le 1/2$ for a Bernoulli($\theta$) variable is stated without the argument $\sigma^2 = \theta(1-\theta) \le 1/4$, maximized at $\theta = 1/2$. The convergences $\hat\sigma_n^2 \to \sigma^2$ and $\hat S_n^2 \to \sigma^2$ are asserted (weak law applied to the i.i.d. terms $(X_i-\theta)^2$) with no proof, and the reason the denominator changes from $n$ to $n-1$ — which is exactly what makes $\mathbf{E}[\hat S_n^2] = \sigma^2$ exactly true once the unknown $\theta$ is replaced by $\hat\Theta_n$ — is stated but not derived.]

This is the only genuinely uncertain reading in the lecture.
2. **$\hat\Theta$ vs. $\hat\theta$.** The lecturer distinguishes the *estimator* (a random variable, capital $\hat\Theta$ or $\hat\Theta_n$) from the *estimate* (a number computed from data $x_1,\dots,x_n$, lowercase $\hat\theta$, e.g. $\hat\theta_{\mathsf{ML}}$, $\hat\theta_{\mathsf{MAP}}$). Both appear side by side on Slide 3; this is preserved exactly.
3. **Slide 5, "$W_i$: i.i.d., mean, 0, variance $\sigma^2$".** The stray comma after "mean" is present on the slide as printed; the intended meaning is "mean 0".
4. **Slide 7, $\hat S_n^2$.** Verified at high magnification: the accent over the capital $S$ is a **hat (circumflex)**, matching the hat on $\hat\sigma_n^2$ two lines above — not a tilde. It is the sample variance with $n-1$ in the denominator. Rendered here as $\hat S_n^2$ throughout, including in the unbiasedness line $\mathbf{E}[\hat S_n^2]=\sigma^2$.
5. **$\alpha$ values on Slide 6.** The three listed values read $0.05$, $0.25$, $0.01$ in the raster. The middle one is unusual for a confidence level (0.025 or 0.1 would be more common), but $0.25$ is what is printed.
