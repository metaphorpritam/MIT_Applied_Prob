## Slide 1 — LECTURE 20: THE CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM (raster p01)

**LECTURE 20**
**THE CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM**

- Readings: Section 5.4

- $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ i.i.d., finite variance $\sigma^2$

- "Standardized" $S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n$:

$$Z_n = \frac{S_n - \mathbf{E}[S_n]}{\sigma_{S_n}} = \frac{S_n - n\mathbf{E}[X]}{\sqrt{n}\,\sigma}$$

  – $\mathbf{E}[Z_n] = 0$, $\qquad \mathrm{var}(Z_n) = 1$

- Let $Z$ be a standard normal r.v.
  (zero mean, unit variance)

- **Theorem:** For every $c$:

$$\mathbf{P}(Z_n \le c) \to \mathbf{P}(Z \le c)$$

- $\mathbf{P}(Z \le c)$ is the standard normal CDF, $\Phi(c)$, available from the normal tables

## Slide 2 — Usefulness / What exactly does it say? / Normal approximation / Moderate $n$ (raster p01)

**Usefulness**

- universal; only means, variances matter
- accurate computational shortcut
- justification of normal models

**What exactly does it say?**

- CDF of $Z_n$ converges to normal CDF
- – not a statement about convergence of PDFs or PMFs

**Normal approximation**

- Treat $Z_n$ as if normal
- – also treat $S_n$ as if normal

**Can we use it when $n$ is "moderate"?**

- Yes, but no nice theorems to this effect
- Symmetry helps a lot

## Slide 4 — The pollster's problem using the CLT (raster p01)

**The pollster's problem using the CLT**

- $f$: fraction of population that "..."

- $i$th (randomly selected) person polled:

$$X_i = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if yes,} \\ 0, & \text{if no.} \end{cases}$$

- $M_n = (X_1 + \cdots + X_n)/n$

- Suppose we want:

$$\mathbf{P}(|M_n - f| \ge .01) \le .05$$

- Event of interest: $|M_n - f| \ge .01$

$$\left| \frac{X_1 + \cdots + X_n - nf}{n} \right| \ge .01$$

$$\left| \frac{X_1 + \cdots + X_n - nf}{\sqrt{n}\,\sigma} \right| \ge \frac{.01\sqrt{n}}{\sigma}$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{P}(|M_n - f| \ge .01) &\approx \mathbf{P}\big(|Z| \ge .01\sqrt{n}/\sigma\big) \\
&\le \mathbf{P}\big(|Z| \ge .02\sqrt{n}\big)
\end{aligned}$$

[DERIVATION GAP: the last inequality uses the fact that for a Bernoulli r.v. $\sigma = \sqrt{f(1-f)} \le 1/2$, so $.01\sqrt{n}/\sigma \ge .02\sqrt{n}$; the slide does not state this step.]

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE]
The pollster's problem itself is left unfinished on the slides: the requirement $\mathbf{P}(|M_n - f| \ge .01) \le .05$ is never solved for $n$. One must still impose $\mathbf{P}(|Z| \ge .02\sqrt{n}) \le .05$, i.e. $2\big(1 - \Phi(.02\sqrt{n})\big) \le .05$, and invert the normal table to find the required sample size $n$. No numerical answer appears on any slide.

## Slide 5 — Apply to binomial / Example (raster p02)

**Apply to binomial**

- Fix $p$, where $0 < p < 1$

- $X_i$: Bernoulli($p$)

- $S_n = X_1 + \cdots + X_n$: Binomial($n, p$)

  – mean $np$, variance $np(1-p)$

- CDF of $\dfrac{S_n - np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}} \longrightarrow$ standard normal

**Example**

- $n = 36$, $p = 0.5$; find $\mathbf{P}(S_n \le 21)$

- Exact answer:

$$\sum_{k=0}^{21} \binom{36}{k} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{36} = 0.8785$$

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE]
The slide poses "find $\mathbf{P}(S_n \le 21)$" for $n = 36$, $p = 0.5$ and gives only the EXACT binomial value $0.8785$. The CLT/normal approximation to this quantity is never carried out numerically on the slides (a deliberate blank gap is left on the slide between the problem statement and the exact answer — the lecturer works it on the board). To complete it: $\mathbf{E}[S_n] = np = 18$, $\sigma_{S_n} = \sqrt{np(1-p)} = \sqrt{9} = 3$, so $\mathbf{P}(S_n \le 21) \approx \mathbf{P}(Z \le (21-18)/3) = \Phi(1) = 0.8413$ without the 1/2 correction, and $\Phi(3.5/3) = \Phi(1.17) = 0.8790$ with it.

[DERIVATION GAP: the numbers $np = 18$ and $\sqrt{np(1-p)} = 3$ used on the next two slides are never displayed on this slide.]

## Slide 6 — The 1/2 correction for binomial approximation (raster p02)

**The 1/2 correction for binomial approximation**

- $\mathbf{P}(S_n \le 21) = \mathbf{P}(S_n < 22)$, because $S_n$ is integer

- Compromise: consider $\mathbf{P}(S_n \le 21.5)$

## Slide 7 — De Moivre–Laplace CLT (for binomial) (raster p02)

**De Moivre–Laplace CLT (for binomial)**

- When the 1/2 correction is used, CLT can also approximate the binomial p.m.f. (not just the binomial CDF)

$$\mathbf{P}(S_n = 19) = \mathbf{P}(18.5 \le S_n \le 19.5)$$

$$18.5 \le S_n \le 19.5 \quad \Longleftrightarrow$$

$$\frac{18.5 - 18}{3} \le \frac{S_n - 18}{3} \le \frac{19.5 - 18}{3} \quad \Longleftrightarrow$$

$$0.17 \le Z_n \le 0.5$$

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{P}(S_n = 19) &\approx \mathbf{P}(0.17 \le Z \le 0.5) \\
&= \mathbf{P}(Z \le 0.5) - \mathbf{P}(Z \le 0.17) \\
&= 0.6915 - 0.5675 \\
&= 0.124
\end{aligned}$$

- Exact answer:

$$\binom{36}{19} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{36} = 0.1251$$

[DERIVATION GAP: the first equality $\mathbf{P}(S_n = 19) = \mathbf{P}(18.5 \le S_n \le 19.5)$ is exact only because $S_n$ is integer-valued; the mean 18 and standard deviation 3 are carried over from the previous example ($n = 36$, $p = 0.5$) without being restated. Also $0.5/3 = 0.1\overline{6}$ is rounded to $0.17$ on the slide.]

## Slide 8 — Poisson vs. normal approximations of the binomial (raster p02)

**Poisson vs. normal approximations of the binomial**

- Poisson arrivals during unit interval equals: sum of $n$ (independent) Poisson arrivals during $n$ intervals of length $1/n$

  – Let $n \to \infty$, apply CLT (??)

  – Poisson = normal (????)

- Binomial($n, p$)

  – $p$ fixed, $n \to \infty$: normal

  – $np$ fixed, $n \to \infty$, $p \to 0$: Poisson

- $p = 1/100$, $n = 100$: Poisson

- $p = 1/10$, $n = 500$: normal

[DERIVATION GAP: the "(??)" and "(????)" mark a deliberate paradox the lecturer resolves orally — the naive argument would conclude that a Poisson r.v. is normal. The resolution (the summands' distribution changes with $n$, so the i.i.d.-fixed-distribution hypothesis of the CLT is violated; equivalently the CLT applies only after $n \to \infty$ with the per-term distribution held fixed) is not written on the slide.]
