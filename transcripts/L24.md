## Slide 2 — Review (raster p01)

**Review**

- **Maximum likelihood estimation**
  - Have model with unknown parameters:
    $X \sim p_X(x;\theta)$
  - Pick $\theta$ that "makes data most likely"
    $$\max_{\theta} \; p_X(x;\theta)$$
  - Compare to Bayesian MAP estimation:
    $$\max_{\theta} \; p_{\Theta\mid X}(\theta \mid x) \quad\text{or}\quad \max_{\theta} \; \frac{p_{X\mid\Theta}(x\mid\theta)\, p_{\Theta}(\theta)}{p_Y(y)}$$

- **Sample mean estimate of** $\theta = \mathbf{E}[X]$
  $$\hat{\Theta}_n = (X_1 + \cdots + X_n)/n$$

- $1-\alpha$ **confidence interval**
  $$\mathbf{P}\big(\hat{\Theta}_n^{-} \le \theta \le \hat{\Theta}_n^{+}\big) \ge 1-\alpha, \quad \forall\, \theta$$

- confidence interval for sample mean
  - let $z$ be s.t. $\Phi(z) = 1 - \alpha/2$
    $$\mathbf{P}\!\left(\hat{\Theta}_n - \frac{z\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \le \theta \le \hat{\Theta}_n + \frac{z\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right) \approx 1-\alpha$$

[DERIVATION GAP: the MAP denominator is written $p_Y(y)$ on the slide, but by the context of the numerator $p_{X\mid\Theta}(x\mid\theta)p_\Theta(\theta)$ the intended normalizer is $p_X(x)$ — an apparent slide typo, transcribed as printed.]

[DERIVATION GAP: the confidence-interval statement for the sample mean is quoted without derivation. The suppressed steps are: the CLT approximation $(\hat\Theta_n-\theta)\sqrt{n}/\sigma \approx N(0,1)$, hence $\mathbf{P}(|\hat\Theta_n-\theta| \le z\sigma/\sqrt n) \approx \Phi(z)-\Phi(-z) = 2\Phi(z)-1 = 1-\alpha$. Also unaddressed: what to do when $\sigma$ is unknown (plug in an estimate / use the t-distribution).]

## Slide 3 — Regression (raster p01)

**Regression**

- Data: $(x_1,y_1),\ (x_2,y_2),\ldots,(x_n,y_n)$
- Model: $y \approx \theta_0 + \theta_1 x$
  $$\min_{\theta_0,\theta_1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \theta_0 - \theta_1 x_i)^2 \qquad (*)$$

- One interpretation:
  $$Y_i = \theta_0 + \theta_1 x_i + W_i, \quad W_i \sim N(0,\sigma^2), \text{ i.i.d.}$$
  - Likelihood function $f_{X,Y\mid\theta}(x,y;\theta)$ is:
    $$c \cdot \exp\left\{ -\frac{1}{2\sigma^2} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \theta_0 - \theta_1 x_i)^2 \right\}$$
  - Take logs, same as $(*)$
  - Least sq. $\leftrightarrow$ pretend $W_i$ i.i.d. normal

[DERIVATION GAP: "Take logs, same as $(*)$" compresses the argument that maximizing $c\cdot\exp\{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum(y_i-\theta_0-\theta_1x_i)^2\}$ over $(\theta_0,\theta_1)$ is equivalent to minimizing $\sum(y_i-\theta_0-\theta_1x_i)^2$; the constant $c$ (the normalization $(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-n/2}$) and the monotonicity of $\log$ are not spelled out.]

## Slide 4 — Linear regression (raster p01)

**Linear regression**

- **Model** $y \approx \theta_0 + \theta_1 x$
  $$\min_{\theta_0,\theta_1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \theta_0 - \theta_1 x_i)^2$$

- **Solution** (set derivatives to zero):
  $$\overline{x} = \frac{x_1 + \cdots + x_n}{n}, \quad \overline{y} = \frac{y_1 + \cdots + y_n}{n}$$
  $$\hat\theta_1 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \overline{x})(y_i - \overline{y})}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i - \overline{x})^2}$$
  $$\hat\theta_0 = \overline{y} - \hat\theta_1 \overline{x}$$

- **Interpretation** of the form of the solution
  - Assume a model $Y = \theta_0 + \theta_1 X + W$
    $W$ independent of $X$, with zero mean
  - Check that
    $$\theta_1 = \frac{\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)}{\operatorname{var}(X)} = \frac{\mathbf{E}\big[(X - \mathbf{E}[X])(Y - \mathbf{E}[Y])\big]}{\mathbf{E}\big[(X - \mathbf{E}[X])^2\big]}$$
  - Solution formula for $\hat\theta_1$ uses natural estimates of the variance and covariance

[DERIVATION GAP: "set derivatives to zero" — the two normal equations $\partial/\partial\theta_0 = 0$ and $\partial/\partial\theta_1 = 0$ and their algebraic solution are omitted; only the final closed forms are given.]

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE]
"Check that $\theta_1 = \dfrac{\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)}{\operatorname{var}(X)} = \dfrac{\mathbf{E}[(X-\mathbf{E}[X])(Y-\mathbf{E}[Y])]}{\mathbf{E}[(X-\mathbf{E}[X])^2]}$" — the verification is left to the student, under the model $Y = \theta_0 + \theta_1 X + W$ with $W$ independent of $X$ and $\mathbf{E}[W]=0$.

## Slide 5 — The world of linear regression (raster p02)

**The world of linear regression**

- **Multiple linear regression:**
  - **data:** $(x_i, x_i', x_i'', y_i)$, $i = 1,\ldots,n$
  - **model:** $y \approx \theta_0 + \theta x + \theta' x' + \theta'' x''$
  - **formulation:**
    $$\min_{\theta,\theta',\theta''} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \theta_0 - \theta x_i - \theta' x_i' - \theta'' x_i'')^2$$

- **Choosing the right variables**
  - model $y \approx \theta_0 + \theta_1 h(x)$
    e.g., $y \approx \theta_0 + \theta_1 x^2$
  - work with data points $(y_i, h(x))$
  - formulation:
    $$\min_{\theta} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \theta_0 - \theta_1 h_1(x_i))^2$$

Footnote on notation: in the multiple-regression minimization the subscript list is printed $\theta,\theta',\theta''$ (the intercept $\theta_0$ is implicitly also a decision variable). In the "choosing the right variables" formulation the slide writes $h(x)$ in the model line but $h_1(x_i)$ inside the sum; both refer to the same chosen transformation of the explanatory variable.

## Slide 7 — Binary hypothesis testing (raster p02)

**Binary hypothesis testing**

- Binary $\theta$; new terminology:
  - **null hypothesis** $H_0$:
    $$X \sim p_X(x; H_0) \qquad [\text{or } f_X(x; H_0)]$$
  - **alternative hypothesis** $H_1$:
    $$X \sim p_X(x; H_1) \qquad [\text{or } f_X(x; H_1)]$$

- Partition the space of possible data vectors
  **Rejection region** $R$:
  reject $H_0$ iff data $\in R$

- Types of errors:
  - **Type I** (**false rejection**, false alarm):
    $H_0$ true, but rejected
    $$\alpha(R) \;=\; \mathbf{P}(X \in R\,; H_0)$$
  - **Type II** (**false acceptance**, missed detection):
    $H_0$ false, but accepted
    $$\beta(R) \;=\; \mathbf{P}(X \notin R\,; H_1)$$

## Slide 8 — Likelihood ratio test (LRT) (raster p02)

**Likelihood ratio test (LRT)**

- Bayesian case (MAP rule): choose $H_1$ if:
  $$\mathbf{P}(H_1 \mid X = x) > \mathbf{P}(H_0 \mid X = x)$$
  or
  $$\frac{\mathbf{P}(X = x \mid H_1)\mathbf{P}(H_1)}{\mathbf{P}(X = x)} > \frac{\mathbf{P}(X = x \mid H_0)\mathbf{P}(H_0)}{\mathbf{P}(X = x)}$$
  or
  $$\frac{\mathbf{P}(X = x \mid H_1)}{\mathbf{P}(X = x \mid H_0)} > \frac{\mathbf{P}(H_0)}{\mathbf{P}(H_1)}$$
  (likelihood ratio test)

- Nonbayesian version: choose $H_1$ if
  $$\frac{\mathbf{P}(X = x; H_1)}{\mathbf{P}(X = x; H_0)} > \xi \quad \text{(discrete case)}$$
  $$\frac{f_X(x; H_1)}{f_X(x; H_0)} > \xi \qquad \text{(continuous case)}$$

- threshold $\xi$ trades off the two types of error
  - choose $\xi$ so that $\mathbf{P}(\text{reject } H_0; H_0) = \alpha$
    (e.g., $\alpha = 0.05$)

Footnote on notation: the threshold symbol is $\xi$ (xi), not $\zeta$ or $\varepsilon$ — resolved from the shape in the raster and from the standard notation in Section 9.3–9.4 of the text.

[DERIVATION GAP: the passage from the MAP comparison to the likelihood-ratio form uses Bayes' rule and cancellation of the common denominator $\mathbf{P}(X=x)$; the slide shows the intermediate line but not the justification, and does not treat ties or the continuous-density analogue of the Bayesian step.]
