## Slide 5 — Combinations (raster p02)

- (in red) $\binom{n}{k}$: number of $k$-element subsets of a given $n$-element set
- Two ways of constructing an ordered sequence of $k$ **distinct** items:
  - Choose the $k$ items one at a time:
    $$n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1) = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!} \ \text{ choices}$$
  - Choose $k$ items, then order them ($k!$ possible orders)
- Hence:
  $$\binom{n}{k} \cdot k! = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!}$$
  (and, in red on the slide)
  $$\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}$$

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE]
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} =$$
(the right-hand side is blank on the slide; intended answer $2^n$, since summing over all subset sizes counts all subsets of an $n$-element set)

[DERIVATION GAP: The equality of the two construction methods (choose-then-order vs. choose-one-at-a-time) is asserted; the slide does not spell out that both count the same set of ordered $k$-tuples of distinct items.]

## Slide 6 — Binomial probabilities (raster p02)

- $n$ independent coin tosses
  - $\mathbf{P}(H) = p$

[UNSOLVED EXAMPLE]
- $\mathbf{P}(HTTHHH) =$
  (blank on the slide; intended answer $p^4(1-p)^2$ — the sequence $HTTHHH$ has 4 heads and 2 tails)

- $\mathbf{P}(\text{sequence}) = p^{\#\ \text{heads}}(1-p)^{\#\ \text{tails}}$

- (in red) $\mathbf{P}(k \text{ heads}) = \displaystyle\sum_{k-\text{head seq.}} \mathbf{P}(\text{seq.})$

$$= (\#\ \text{of } k\text{--head seqs.}) \cdot p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$$

$$= \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} \qquad \text{(in red on the slide)}$$

[DERIVATION GAP: The step from the sum over $k$-head sequences to (number of sequences) × (common probability) uses the fact that every $k$-head sequence has the same probability $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$; and the count of $k$-head sequences is identified with $\binom{n}{k}$ without restating the subset-of-toss-positions argument.]

## Slide 8 — Partitions (raster p02)

- 52-card deck, dealt to 4 players
- Find $\mathbf{P}(\text{each gets an ace})$
- Outcome: a partition of the 52 cards
  - number of outcomes:
    $$\frac{52!}{13!\,13!\,13!\,13!}$$
- Count number of ways of distributing the four aces: $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2$
- Count number of ways of dealing the remaining 48 cards
  $$\frac{48!}{12!\,12!\,12!\,12!}$$
- Answer:
  $$\frac{4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot \dfrac{48!}{12!\,12!\,12!\,12!}}{\dfrac{52!}{13!\,13!\,13!\,13!}}$$

[DERIVATION GAP: The multinomial count $52!/(13!)^4$ for the number of partitions into four 13-card hands is stated without derivation. The ace-distribution count is written on the slide as $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2$ (the trailing $\cdot 1$ is simply omitted): the first ace can go to any of 4 players, the second to any of the remaining 3, the third to either of the remaining 2, the fourth to the one remaining player — so the count is $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 4! = 24$. The slide does not spell out this stage-by-stage reasoning, nor does it simplify the final ratio.]
