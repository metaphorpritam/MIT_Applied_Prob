## Slide 7 — Probability axioms (raster p02)

**Probability axioms**

- **Event:** a subset of the sample space
- Probability is assigned to events

*(horizontal rule on slide)*

**Axioms:**

1. **Nonnegativity:** $\mathbf{P}(A) \ge 0$
2. **Normalization:** $\mathbf{P}(\Omega) = 1$
3. **Additivity:** If $A \cap B = \varnothing$, then $\mathbf{P}(A \cup B) = \mathbf{P}(A) + \mathbf{P}(B)$ *(axiom 3 shown in red)*

*(horizontal rule on slide)*

- $$\mathbf{P}(\{s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_k\}) = \mathbf{P}(\{s_1\}) + \cdots + \mathbf{P}(\{s_k\})$$
  $$= \mathbf{P}(s_1) + \cdots + \mathbf{P}(s_k)$$

[DERIVATION GAP: the extension of finite additivity from two disjoint sets (Axiom 3) to $k$ disjoint singletons is asserted without the inductive argument; also the notational shorthand $\mathbf{P}(\{s_i\}) = \mathbf{P}(s_i)$ is introduced silently.]

- Axiom 3 needs strengthening
- Do weird sets have probabilities?

[DERIVATION GAP: "Axiom 3 needs strengthening" — the countable-additivity strengthening is only stated later (Slide 11); no justification is given here for why finite additivity is insufficient. "Do weird sets have probabilities?" alludes to non-measurable sets with no explanation.]

## Slide 9 — Discrete uniform law (raster p03)

**Discrete uniform law**

- Let all outcomes be equally likely
- Then,

$$\mathbf{P}(A) = \frac{\text{number of elements of } A}{\text{total number of sample points}}$$

*(this displayed formula is shown in red on the slide)*

- Computing probabilities $\equiv$ counting
- Defines fair coins, fair dice, well-shuffled decks

## Slide 11 — Probability law: Ex. w/countably infinite sample space (raster p03)

**Probability law: Ex. w/countably infinite sample space**

- Sample space: $\{1, 2, \ldots\}$
  - We are given $\mathbf{P}(n) = 2^{-n}$, $n = 1, 2, \ldots$
  - Find $\mathbf{P}(\text{outcome is even})$

[FIGURE: A stem (lollipop) plot of the probability mass on the positive integers. Vertical axis is a plain arrow pointing up, horizontal axis a plain arrow pointing right; **both axes are completely unlabeled — there are no axis names, no tick marks, and no numeric labels anywhere on this plot** (the values must be read off the accompanying text $\mathbf{P}(n)=2^{-n}$). Four short dotted vertical stems rise from the horizontal axis at equally spaced positions (implicitly $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$), each capped by a small pale-blue-filled circular marker. Heights decrease rapidly left to right (implicitly $1/2$, $1/4$, $1/8$, $1/16$), illustrating geometric decay; the axis extends to the right past the fourth stem with no further stems drawn. | raster/L01_p03.png]

$$\mathbf{P}(\{2,4,6,\ldots\}) \;=\; \mathbf{P}(2) + \mathbf{P}(4) + \cdots \;=\; \frac{1}{2^2} + \frac{1}{2^4} + \frac{1}{2^6} + \cdots \;=\; \frac{1}{3}$$

[DERIVATION GAP: the geometric series $\sum_{k\ge 1} (1/4)^k = (1/4)/(1 - 1/4) = 1/3$ is evaluated in one step; the summation formula is not shown.]

- **Countable additivity axiom** *(red on slide)* (needed for this calculation):
  If $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ are disjoint events, then:

$$\mathbf{P}(A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \cdots) = \mathbf{P}(A_1) + \mathbf{P}(A_2) + \cdots$$
