## Slide 6 — Geometric PMF (raster p02)

- $X$: number of independent coin tosses until first head
  $$p_X(k) = (1-p)^{k-1} p, \qquad k = 1, 2, \ldots$$
  $$\mathbf{E}[X] = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k\, p_X(k) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k(1-p)^{k-1} p$$

[DERIVATION GAP: the infinite sum for $\mathbf{E}[X]$ is written but not evaluated here; its value $1/p$ is obtained later on the Total Expectation theorem slide by a different (recursive) argument.]

## Slide 7 — Total Expectation theorem (raster p02)

- Partition of sample space into disjoint events $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_n$

[FIGURE: Venn-style diagram. A rectangle (wider than tall) represents the sample space. It is divided into three regions by two curves: one curve runs from the left edge down/right toward the bottom edge, and another runs from the top edge down to the bottom edge, so the rectangle is split into an upper-left region labeled $A_1$, a lower-left region labeled $A_2$, and a lower-right region labeled $A_3$ (the three labels are printed inside their regions, $A_1$ near the top-left corner, $A_2$ near the bottom-left, $A_3$ near the bottom-right). A grey-shaded ellipse, tilted slightly, is drawn across the middle of the rectangle and straddles all three regions; it represents the event $B$ (the ellipse itself carries no printed label in the raster). | raster/L06_p02.png]

$$\mathbf{P}(B) = \mathbf{P}(A_1)\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_1) + \cdots + \mathbf{P}(A_n)\mathbf{P}(B \mid A_n)$$
$$p_X(x) = \mathbf{P}(A_1) p_{X|A_1}(x) + \cdots + \mathbf{P}(A_n) p_{X|A_n}(x)$$
$$\mathbf{E}[X] = \mathbf{P}(A_1)\mathbf{E}[X \mid A_1] + \cdots + \mathbf{P}(A_n)\mathbf{E}[X \mid A_n]$$

- Geometric example:
  $$A_1 : \{X = 1\}, \qquad A_2 : \{X > 1\}$$
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \mathbf{E}[X] = \;& \mathbf{P}(X = 1)\mathbf{E}[X \mid X = 1] \\
  &+ \mathbf{P}(X > 1)\mathbf{E}[X \mid X > 1]
  \end{aligned}$$
- Solve to get $\mathbf{E}[X] = 1/p$

[DERIVATION GAP: the slide says "Solve to get $\mathbf{E}[X]=1/p$" without showing the substitutions $\mathbf{P}(X=1)=p$, $\mathbf{P}(X>1)=1-p$, $\mathbf{E}[X\mid X=1]=1$, and — by memorylessness — $\mathbf{E}[X\mid X>1] = 1 + \mathbf{E}[X]$, nor the resulting algebra.]
