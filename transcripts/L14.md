[FIGURE: Timeline diagram at the top of the slide. The picture illustrates partitioning continuous time into intervals of durations $t_1, t_2, t_3$ plus a very small interval $\delta$; arrival counts in disjoint intervals are the quantities discussed in the bullets. | raster/L14_p01.png]

- **Time homogeneity:**
  $P(k,\tau)=$ Prob. of $k$ arrivals in interval of duration $\tau$

- Numbers of arrivals in disjoint time intervals are **independent**

- **Small interval probabilities:**
  For VERY small $\delta$:

$$P(k,\delta)\approx\begin{cases}1-\lambda\delta, & \text{if } k=0;\\ \lambda\delta, & \text{if } k=1;\\ 0, & \text{if } k>1.\end{cases}$$

  - $\lambda$: "arrival rate"

## Slide 4 — PMF of Number of Arrivals $N$ (raster p01)

[FIGURE: The same timeline diagram as Slide 3, reproduced pixel-for-pixel at the top of this slide: horizontal time axis with origin $0$ at the left and a rightward arrowhead labeled "Time"; eleven bold "x" arrival marks on the axis; four double-headed interval brackets above the axis delimited by short vertical bars, labeled left to right $t_1$ (widest, 3 arrivals), $t_2$ (narrower, adjacent to $t_1$, 1 arrival), $t_3$ (wide, 3 arrivals) and $\delta$ (very narrow, at the right, 1 arrival), with isolated x's in the unbracketed gaps and 2 trailing x's at the far right. See the Slide 3 figure description for full detail. | raster/L14_p01.png]

- Finely discretize $[0,t]$: approximately Bernoulli
- $N_t$ (of discrete approximation): binomial
- Taking $\delta\to 0$ (or $n\to\infty$) gives:

$$P(k,\tau)=\frac{(\lambda\tau)^k e^{-\lambda\tau}}{k!},\qquad k=0,1,\ldots$$

- $\mathbf{E}[N_t]\;=\;\lambda t,\qquad\qquad \mathrm{var}(N_t)\;=\;\lambda t$

[DERIVATION GAP: The slide states that the binomial pmf of the discretized process converges to the Poisson pmf as $\delta\to 0$ with $n=t/\delta$, $p=\lambda\delta$, but the limiting computation $\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}\to (\lambda t)^k e^{-\lambda t}/k!$ is not carried out on the slides.]

[DERIVATION GAP: $\mathbf{E}[N_t]=\lambda t$ and $\mathrm{var}(N_t)=\lambda t$ are asserted without derivation (they follow as limits of the binomial mean $np$ and variance $np(1-p)$).]

## Slide 6 — Interarrival Times (raster p02)

- $Y_k$ time of $k$th arrival
- **Erlang** distribution:

$$f_{Y_k}(y)=\frac{\lambda^k y^{k-1} e^{-\lambda y}}{(k-1)!},\qquad y\ge 0$$

[FIGURE: Plot of the Erlang probability density family, drawn inside a thin black rectangular frame, with the caption "Image by MIT OpenCourseWare." centered below the frame. Both axes are drawn as medium-blue arrows: a short vertical arrow (up) and a long horizontal arrow (right). The vertical-axis label, set in small italic type at the top left, reads $f_{I_r}(l)$ and the horizontal-axis label at the right of the arrow is the matching single italic letter $l$ — i.e. the OCW figure uses its own variable letter, NOT the $y$ of the slide's formula, and the shape parameter in the figure is $r$ (playing the role of $k$ in the slide's formula). The origin is marked $0$ just below-left of the axis crossing. A boxed legend with a light-gray drop shadow sits in the upper-middle/right of the frame, listing three colored line samples: dark red/brown "r = 1", orange "r = 2", green "r = 3". Curves: the $r=1$ curve rises vertically from the origin to a positive maximum on the vertical axis and then decays monotonically to near zero (exponential density); the $r=2$ curve starts at $0$ at the origin, rises to a peak at roughly one-quarter of the plotted range (the tallest of the two unimodal curves), then decays; the $r=3$ curve starts at $0$, rises more slowly to a lower, broader peak near the middle of the plotted range, and decays most slowly, remaining the highest curve at the right edge. The $r=1$ and $r=2$ curves cross once (early); the $r=2$ and $r=3$ curves cross once (past the $r=2$ peak). Peaks move right and flatten as $r$ increases. | raster/L14_p02.png]

- Time of first arrival ($k=1$): **exponential**: $f_{Y_1}(y)=\lambda e^{-\lambda y},\quad y\ge 0$
  - **Memoryless** property: The time to the next arrival is independent of the past

[DERIVATION GAP: The Erlang density is stated without derivation; the slides do not show the argument (e.g. $P(y\le Y_k\le y+\delta)=P(k-1 \text{ arrivals in } [0,y])\cdot\lambda\delta$) that yields it.]
