§0   How to use this reference

Sources: the seven study notes G1–G7 and the question bank Q · Bertsekas–Tsitsiklis (B&T) for every in-syllabus result · the Chen–Blitzstein Probability Cheatsheet v2.0 for the enrichment topics of §3–§4 · corpus counts from computes/rf_s0.py. A bare "§N" inside a citation such as "G3 §4" always means that note's section N; every reference to the textbook carries the "B&T" prefix.

This page is the eighth and last, and it is the only one that teaches nothing. The seven notes G1–G7 are a course: they build the subject in order, across 28 numbered teaching sections, deriving every result before using it. Reading them is a linear activity. Looking something up is not — and that is the gap this page fills. It has two halves, and they serve two different readers.

The lookup half (§1–§2) is for the reader who already did the course and now wants one number, one density, or one identity, fast. An audit of these notes against a standard one-page cheatsheet reached a blunt verdict: coverage is strong — of 189 checked items only 3 were in the 6.041 syllabus and genuinely absent — but "the distribution facts exist but are scattered across four tables in four different notes." That is a retrieval problem, not a content problem, and §1 and §2 solve it by consolidation. §1 is one table of every named distribution in the course — PMF or PDF, range, mean, variance, the mechanism that generates it, and the note that derives it. §2 is the master formula sheet: the expectation, variance, conditioning, transform, limit, and inference identities of all seven notes on one surface, grouped by what you are trying to do rather than by which week they were taught.

The enrichment half (§3–§4) is for a different reader: someone arriving from Harvard's Stat 110 or from self-study, who has met machinery that 6.041 simply does not use. The same audit classified 32 cheatsheet items as out-of-syllabus rather than missing — chiefly moment generating functions, order statistics, and a zoo of named distributions and identities wider than B&T needs. Those are collected here, stated properly and connected back to the in-course machinery they generalize, so that a reader coming the other way is not left guessing whether an unfamiliar symbol was something these notes forgot.

Nothing on this page is derived from scratch. Every in-syllabus entry carries a pointer to the note section that does the work — that is where the argument, the intuition, the worked examples, and the figures live. Treat an entry here as an index card, and the cited section as the thing the card indexes. (The enrichment entries of §3–§4 have no such parent section by construction, so they carry short self-contained justifications instead, plus a B&T or cheatsheet citation.)

Recipe — looking something up.
  1. You want a distribution. Its PMF or PDF, its range, its mean or variance, or "which family is this mechanism?" → §1, the consolidated distribution table. It covers the discrete families of G2 §1 and G2 §3, the continuous families of G3 §1, the Erlang and the arrival-process families of G4 §3–§4, and the normal in the form used by the CLT in G6 §2.
  2. You want an identity. An expectation or variance rule, a conditioning law, a convolution, a bound, an estimator or a test statistic → §2, the master formula sheet. Each line names its home section: linearity and variance rules in G2 §2–§3, joint and derived distributions in G3 §2 and G3 §4, iterated expectation and random sums in G4 §1–§2, steady-state and absorption equations in G5 §3–§4, inequalities and the CLT in G6 §1–§2, estimators, confidence intervals and tests in G7 §1–§4.
  3. You do not know which tool you need. Do not start here. Start at the note itself — its §0 orientation says what the note is for, and its §5 summary is a one-screen recap. This page assumes you already know the name of the thing you are hunting.
  4. You met a symbol these notes never used. a moment generating function $M_X(t)$, an order statistic $X_{(k)}$, a named family that appears nowhere in G1–G7 → §3 and §4, the beyond-syllabus half.
  5. You want to practise instead of look up. That is the previous page, Q · Question Bank: 150 original questions with fully worked solutions, filtered by difficulty and by section.
Gotcha — §3 and §4 are not part of MIT 6.041. They are outside the syllabus of this course and outside B&T's treatment of it. Nothing in them is examinable in 6.041, nothing in the seven notes depends on them, and no exercise in the question bank needs them. They are here because this site is public and some of its readers are taking a different course (Stat 110 covers this material) or reading on their own with a broader cheatsheet in hand — and because it is genuinely useful to be told "this is a real topic, it is just not this course's topic" rather than to wonder whether you missed a lecture. If you are working through 6.041, you are not behind for skipping §3 and §4 entirely. The converse warning also holds: do not import a §3 technique into a 6.041 solution and expect credit for it — the graded path to every in-syllabus result is the one the cited note takes.
Interpretation — why consolidation is worth a page of its own. A teaching note and a reference table are optimized against different costs. A note minimizes the effort of understanding a result the first time, which means derivation, motivation, figures, and one idea at a time, spread over the week it belongs to. A table minimizes the time to re-find a result you have already understood, which means everything comparable placed side by side, with the derivations stripped out. The seven notes are excellent at the first job and structurally bad at the second — the geometric mean $1/p$ is derived in G2 §3 and the exponential mean $1/\lambda$ in G3 §1, two notes and several weeks apart, even though the whole point is that they are the same fact at two time resolutions (G4 §3). Putting the two on adjacent rows of one table is not a summary of the notes; it is a different, complementary object.
